# Experience Decision — BlaxkVERSE

**Status:** decided · **Date:** 2026-10-15 · **Owner:** creative direction for BLAXK BYTE TECHNOLOGIES / Blaxk

> This document is the gate required by §13A of the master specification. It records three runnable prototypes, how they were measured and scored, which one wins, and the interaction rules the production build must follow. Three concepts were built and run. None of them was a static mock-up.

---

## 1. What was actually built

Three complete, runnable prototypes. Each has a hero, working navigation with keyboard and touch support, at least two real project transitions, a contact exit, a reduced-motion/low-power fallback, and its own instrumentation.

| | Concept A | Concept B | Concept C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Spatial Constellation | Source Reactor | Infinite Signal |
| **File** | [`prototypes/concept-a.html`](../prototypes/concept-a.html) | [`prototypes/concept-b.html`](../prototypes/concept-b.html) | [`prototypes/concept-c.html`](../prototypes/concept-c.html) |
| **Core idea** | Projects are places in a navigable field; scrolling moves a camera through the constellation | The interface is an engineering instrument — projects are modules in a rack, and source is a readable artifact on the same stage | A cinematic editorial journey; horizontal-feeling chapters driven by ordinary vertical scrolling, with type as the primary material |
| **Navigation** | Floating morphing "Command Beacon" opening a radial orbit + accessible list panel; `Cmd/Ctrl+K` parity | Retractable command rail with data-driven search across projects, technologies and commands; roving-tabindex module rack | Minimal signal dial (ticks + hand) plus an always-visible conventional top nav that is never replaced |
| **Motion driver** | Canvas field, camera rotation + depth walk tied to native scroll | Native scroll over sticky stage, transforming between three faces of the same module | Native scroll over a sticky viewport, translating one horizontal track |
| **Canvas cost** | Yes — 2D canvas field (mitigated, see perf report) | None | None |

Reference screenshots for every state are in `evidence/shots/` (18 images: 3 concepts × {desktop entry, desktop mid, desktop late, mobile entry, mobile mid, reduced-motion desktop}).

## 2. Measurements

Full method and all figures: [`docs/performance-report.md`](./performance-report.md) (generated by `tools/report.mjs` from `evidence/measurements.json`). Summary — median of 3 passes per configuration, 18 configurations total, **zero console errors in any run**:

| Concept | desktop (full) | mobile (full) | low-end (full) | worst p95 | long tasks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** Spatial Constellation | **60** | 60 | 60 | 16.8 ms | 0 |
| **B** Source Reactor | 55 | 60 | 58 | 33.3 ms | 0 |
| **C** Infinite Signal | 50 | 60 | 56 | 33.4 ms | 0 |

*low-end = CPU throttled 6× via CDP. Load cost ≈ 31 KB, zero external requests, zero third-party JS.*

**The one uncomfortable result, stated plainly:** Concept A is the *fastest* prototype and Concept C is the *slowest* on a desktop-class pass. A 50 fps median is about 20% of dropped frames during heavy scroll, which is a real cost — it is not hidden here and it is addressed in the production build (§6).

## 3. Scoring

Scored out of 100 against the weights the specification fixes: originality 25, navigation clarity 20, motion quality 20, mobile/performance 15, accessibility 10, engineering maintainability 10.

| Criterion | Max | A | B | C | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Originality** | 25 | **24** | 19 | 22 | A is the most unusual idea on the page — a constellation you traverse, where links encode real shared themes. B's instrument metaphor is coherent but a rack-and-stage console is a known pattern. C is a strong, disciplined editorial treatment with genuinely cinematic chapter cuts, though "big type over black" is a familiar register. |
| **Navigation clarity** | 20 | 14 | **18** | 16 | B wins decisively: a persistent rail, a real search index over projects *and* technologies, and a standard roving-tabindex tablist for the module rack. A's radial orbit is memorable but a first-time visitor must learn it. C's dial is minimal; its saving grace is that the conventional top nav is never removed. |
| **Motion quality** | 20 | 15 | 17 | **19** | C's masked reveals and chapter cuts are the most cinematic and best paced. B's face transformations are crisp and purposeful. A's field is atmospheric but the camera move is less legible — it reads as background more than as travel. |
| **Mobile / performance** | 15 | **12** | **13** | **13** | A holds 60 fps everywhere but only *after* the compositing fix (24 → 60), and it is the only concept that needs a canvas. B and C have no canvas at all, so their mobile margin is wider. |
| **Accessibility** | 10 | 8 | **9** | 8 | All three have skip links, focus traps with focus restoration, `Esc` handling, `Cmd/Ctrl+K` parity and full reduced-motion paths. B is ahead because the module rack is a real tablist with arrow keys, and because the code artifact carries its path/commit metadata as text rather than as decoration. |
| **Engineering maintainability** | 10 | 8 | **9** | 8 | B's data-driven rail and module rack come from the project records directly; adding a project is a data change. A's field and C's track both need per-scene tuning when content changes. |
| **Total** | **100** | **81** | **85** | **86** | |

**Winner: Concept C — Infinite Signal (86/100).**

**Runner-up by one point: Concept B — Source Reactor (85/100).** That margin is inside the noise of subjective scoring, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise. B is the better *instrument*; C is the better *film*. C is chosen because this brief is explicitly a flagship brand experience whose job is to make a first-time visitor understand the company and feel something in five seconds — and because C's story is carried by typography and real content, which is the most honest substrate for a portfolio whose central claim is "real engineering evidence earns trust."

**Concept A is not discarded.** It is the most original idea in the set, and A's constellation is reused in production as the **Work field** — a secondary visual on `/work` with the readable list as the primary path. A was rejected as the *primary* experience for one reason: it makes the strongest first impression require learning an interaction, and the specification's first acceptance criterion is that a visitor identifies the company and a contact path in five seconds.

This is the synthesis, not a copy of C:

| From | Adopted into production as |
|---|---|
| **C** | The chapter/journey narrative spine; the masked type reveals; the signal dial as a secondary instrument; chapter numbering as a wayfinding motif; the "Explore normally" escape hatch as a first-class button rather than a hidden setting |
| **B** | The navigation model: persistent rail + real search over projects, technologies and commands. The `SourceChamber` (three faces of one artifact: story / architecture / source) becomes the Project X-Ray component. The roving-tabindex rack becomes the project selector. |
| **A** | The constellation as the `/work` field, with links drawn only from real shared themes — plus A's hard-won compositing lesson, which is now a rule: **no `backdrop-filter` or blend mode over any animating layer.** |

## 5. Interaction rules the production build must obey

These are binding. They come from the specification's hard rules and from what the prototypes taught us.

1. **Native scrolling is the foundation.** No wheel-event trapping, no scroll-jacking, no smooth-scroll library. Scroll-linked choreography uses `requestAnimationFrame`, reads `scrollY`, and writes only `transform`/`opacity`. Deep links, browser back/forward, `Ctrl+F`, text selection and anchors keep working in every scene.
2. **A conventional navigation path always exists.** The chapter dial is an addition to the top nav, never a replacement. On touch it is a full-screen sheet with large targets.
3. **Exactly three motion modes**, shareable through the URL and honouring the OS preference: `full` (default), `reduced` (`prefers-reduced-motion`), `static` (`?motion=off`, the "Explore normally" route). All three render **the same content**; only motion and depth reduce. Every mode is tested by the harness.
4. **No content without a DOM equivalent.** Anything drawn to canvas is duplicated as real text with real links. This is why A's core content lives in a readable list and B's code has a copyable text form.
5. **No compositing over animating layers.** No `backdrop-filter`, no `mix-blend-mode` on any element that overlaps a canvas or a scroll-animated layer. This single rule was worth 36 fps in Concept A.
6. **Focus is a feature.** Every overlay traps focus and restores it to its trigger on close; `Esc` closes everything; `Cmd/Ctrl+K` reaches every destination; focus-visible styling is never removed.
7. **Reduced motion is not reduced quality.** The low-motion route is art-directed, not stripped — it is the same design with the camera parked.
8. **Cursor proximity may reveal metadata, never hide controls.** No control may depend on pointer position.
9. **Source is inert.** Code is rendered as highlighted text. Nothing from a repository is ever executed in a visitor's browser.
10. **One tasteful WebGL moment, not a WebGL site.** The production build uses at most one dynamic 3D scene, capability-gated with a static poster fallback, paused when offscreen.

## 6. How production addresses the measured weaknesses

| Weakness found | Production response |
|---|---|
| C lost ~20% of frames on desktop during heavy scroll | The production track animates **only `transform`** on a single composited layer, pre-measures chapter offsets once per resize (never per frame), and is capped by an adaptive quality tier. Identical harness re-run against the built site. |
| A's 24 fps compositing disaster | Promoted to a global rule (§5.5) and enforced by a grep-able check in the audit script. |
| Prototype HUD covered content on mobile | The production instrument panel is **opt-in** and off by default; nothing diagnostic is ever shown to a normal visitor. |
| No real-device testing possible here | The report states this limit instead of implying coverage. A `docs/performance-report.md` section lists exactly what remains unverified on hardware. |
