Good fits and honest mismatches

Usually a good fit

  • An application that needs to exist and be maintained, not a one-off page.
  • Automation where a person is currently the integration layer.
  • AI features that must keep keys server-side and behave sanely when the model is down.
  • Tooling that has to work offline, or on data that cannot leave the building.
  • A prototype whose job is to answer a feasibility question.

Usually not a fit

  • Work that needs a large team or a certified compliance regime.
  • Anything requiring a claim that cannot be evidenced — inflated metrics, fake reviews, misleading product behaviour.
  • Security work framed as a certified penetration test.
  • Projects that need a price before anyone has read the requirements.

Being clear about this early saves both sides a conversation that was never going to end well.
